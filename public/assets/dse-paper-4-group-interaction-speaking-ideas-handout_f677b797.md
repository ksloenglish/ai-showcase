# DSE Paper 4 Group Interaction Speaking-Ideas Handout

## What this makes

This makes a print-ready Word handout for students preparing for **HKDSE English Language Paper 4, Part A: Group Interaction**. It provides short, balanced ideas for use in a post-discussion debriefing; it does not include an answer key or teacher notes.

## What you need

- One clear image, scan or PDF of an HKDSE English Language Paper 4 topic sheet.
- An optional Word template if you need a particular school layout.
- An optional language level: the default is CEFR C1, but you may request B1 or B2.

## How to use it

1. Prepare the topic sheet and, if needed, a Word template.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Read through the result before giving it to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create a print-ready Word idea-generation handout for students preparing for HKDSE English Language Paper 4, Part A: Group Interaction.

### Materials
I have provided: [A CLEAR IMAGE, SCAN OR PDF OF ONE HKDSE ENGLISH LANGUAGE PAPER 4 TOPIC SHEET].
Use only information you can read from this material for ideas labelled “(from text)”. Do not invent, alter or silently omit source content.

### Task
Read the topic sheet and create a concise handout for a debriefing after the group discussion. Work on Part A only and ignore Part B. Identify the three specific bullet points under “You may want to talk about:”; do not make a section for “anything else you think is important”.

### Requirements
1. Make one Heading 2 section for each of the three specific Part A subtopics. Keep the exact wording of each bullet point as closely as possible, changing only what is necessary to make it a noun phrase.
2. Give exactly five numbered ideas for every section. Each idea should be about 10 words or fewer. Prefer a concise noun phrase or short verb phrase; use a one-clause sentence only where needed for clarity. Do not add explanations or examples within an idea.
3. Start each section with all relevant ideas drawn from the reading text. Label each of these points “(from text)”, use the original wording where possible, and ensure each fact remains linked to the same subject as in the source.
4. Place all “(from text)” ideas consecutively before any original ideas. Before finalising, identify their positions in the source text and check that they appear in the handout in the same ascending order. Do not place an original idea above or between them.
5. Add original, distinct ideas until every section contains five ideas. Make all original ideas suitable and valid for the Hong Kong context. If the topic is specifically Hong Kong-related, use well-known local examples where helpful and verify them with credible sources.
6. For every subtopic beginning with “whether”, include points supporting both sides of the discussion. For a subtopic that asks students to choose from a fixed set of options, keep all five points within that set; use comparison, selection criteria or an overall judgement rather than adding another option.
7. Use natural, speakable British English at up to [CEFR C1]. If I specify B1 or B2, strictly adjust the vocabulary and sentence complexity to that level.
8. Create a `.docx` file ready for printing. Use a centred Heading 1 title, Heading 2 subtopic headings, Calibri or Times New Roman at 12pt, standard A4 margins and 1.15 line spacing. Do not add headers, footers, dates, references or extra commentary.
9. Set the title in this order of priority:
   - If the page header clearly shows a DSE year and topic number, use: “Useful Ideas for [YEAR]-DSE English Language Paper 4 Topic [X.X]”.
   - If they are absent but a descriptive title is clearly shown, use: “Useful Ideas for [TITLE]”.
   - If neither is shown, use: “Useful Ideas for the Speaking Topic about [TOPIC]”.
   Never guess a DSE year or topic number.

### Output
Provide a print-ready `.docx` handout containing the title and three subtopic sections, each with exactly five numbered ideas. Use British English.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- the handout uses Part A only and has exactly the first three specific discussion subtopics;
- each source-text idea is accurate, has “(from text)” at the end, and describes the correct person, place, subject or option from the source;
- all source-text ideas appear first in each section and match the source-text order;
- every section has exactly five concise, distinct ideas, and every “whether” section includes both sides;
- original ideas are suitable for Hong Kong students and any Hong Kong examples have been checked;
- the title follows the stated priority order, and the Word document has the requested layout with no headers, footers, dates or extra commentary.

If you cannot read an attached file, create a Word document, verify an example or check the layout, say so before proceeding. Offer the closest useful alternative, such as an editable Markdown or plain-text version to paste into Word. Do not claim to have completed something you cannot do.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Check that it covers **Part A only** and has three sections, not four.
- Check that all source-text points are marked, accurately worded and placed first in source order.
- Check that there are five short, distinct ideas in every section, with both sides represented in “whether” questions.
- Open the Word file and check that the title, headings and numbered lists are readable and suitable for printing.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Ask it for an editable Markdown or plain-text version instead. You can then paste it into your own Word template.

## What you can change

You may change `[CEFR C1]` to **B1** or **B2**, and you may add a Word template for your school layout. Keep the five-idea structure, Part A focus, source-text labels and source-order rule unchanged because they protect accuracy and make the handout easier for students to use.
