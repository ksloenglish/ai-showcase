# Print-Friendly Educational Infographic

## What this makes

This prompt creates a **clear, modern, single-page infographic** for secondary-school students. It keeps the teacher’s wording exactly as supplied and presents it in an engaging layout that is economical to print.

## What you need

- The exact title, headings, and text that must appear in the infographic.
- The learner group, topic, intended page size, orientation, and language variety.
- An optional school logo, colour preference, or editable template if you need a particular visual identity.
- Any limits on illustrations, symbols, or use of colour.

## How to use it

1. Prepare the material listed above. Paste the text in its final, checked form.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Check the wording, layout, and print appearance before sharing it with students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create a print-friendly educational infographic for [LEARNERS AND CLASSROOM CONTEXT].

### Materials
I have provided: [PASTE OR ATTACH THE EXACT TITLE, HEADINGS, BULLETS, AND ANY SCHOOL BRANDING OR TEMPLATE].
Use only information you can read from these materials. Do not invent, summarise, reword, rearrange, silently omit, or add any text. Preserve the supplied wording, spelling, punctuation, headings, and order exactly.

### Task
Create a single-page infographic on [PAGE SIZE, ORIENTATION, AND OUTPUT FORMAT] for [TOPIC]. It should help students read and discuss the supplied ideas quickly and confidently. Design it for [PRINT / DIGITAL DISPLAY / BOTH], using [BRITISH ENGLISH / OTHER REQUIRED VARIETY].

### Requirements
1. Use a compact title banner that occupies less than 20% of the page height. Give most of the page to the educational content.
2. Divide the supplied content into clearly separated, consistently styled sections. Keep the supplied heading-and-bullet order exactly. Use a clean, highly legible sans-serif typeface. Use a minimum print size of [12 PT, OR OTHER REQUIREMENT] for body text where the content allows.
3. Set text in balanced blocks with consistent line lengths, comfortable spacing, and no large awkward gaps at the ends of lines. Do not clip, overlap, crowd, duplicate, or hide any words.
4. Give each key educational term a restrained visual emphasis, such as bold type, an accent colour, a small icon, or a thin underline. Keep the emphasis consistent across all sections.
5. Use a vibrant but professional, teen-appropriate colour palette: [COLOUR PREFERENCES]. Use accent colours mainly for headings, borders, icons, line art, and highlights. Use white or extremely pale backgrounds. Do not use large dark or heavily saturated background fills.
6. Add a small number of relevant, stylised illustrations that support the topic. Use clean lines and light shading. Keep illustrations secondary to the text, free of words and logos, and positioned so they never interfere with reading.
7. Make the page economical to print. Avoid dense textures, large solid-colour areas, heavy dark blocks, excessive ink coverage, decorative clutter, and background images behind text.
8. Keep practical print margins around all edges. Use strong contrast between the text and its background so the content remains readable when printed.
9. Do not add captions, quotations, facts, examples, logos, dates, headings, icons containing text, or other written content beyond the material I supplied. If a school logo is supplied, use it only as instructed.

### Output
Provide [A PRINT-READY IMAGE / AN EDITABLE SVG OR HTML/CSS LAYOUT / OTHER REQUESTED FORMAT] for one single-page infographic. Include a brief plain-text layout summary that states the page size, orientation, colour palette, section order, and illustration placement. Use [BRITISH ENGLISH / OTHER REQUIRED VARIETY] for any unavoidable interface or file-label wording, but do not add text to the infographic itself beyond the supplied material.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- every title, heading, bullet point, and item of punctuation exactly matches the supplied material, with nothing added, removed, rewritten, or moved out of order;
- the title banner uses less than 20% of the page height, all supplied sections are visible, and no text is clipped, overlapped, duplicated, or placed on a low-contrast background;
- the layout has balanced line lengths, clear hierarchy, practical print margins, and no large unnecessary blank areas;
- accent colours are used sparingly, backgrounds are pale or white, and the design does not rely on large solid fills or heavy ink coverage;
- illustrations are relevant, cleanly styled, secondary to the information, and contain no extra words, logos, or distracting detail; and
- the final page is suitable for [LEARNER AGE OR YEAR GROUP] and the requested use.

If you cannot read an attached file, create the requested image or editable file, ensure exact text rendering, or verify the final layout, say so before proceeding. Offer the closest useful alternative, such as an editable SVG or HTML/CSS layout, a Markdown layout plan, or a printer-friendly background with exact text-placement instructions. Do not claim that the text or layout has been checked if you cannot verify it.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Compare every word, heading, bullet, and punctuation mark with your original text. This is especially important when text has been placed inside an image.
- Check that the title is compact, all sections are readable at normal viewing distance, and the page has enough margins for printing.
- Print one test copy if possible. Check that pale backgrounds still separate sections, colours remain clear, and the sheet does not use unnecessary amounts of ink.
- Confirm that any illustrations support the topic and do not distract students from the key ideas.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Ask it for an editable SVG or HTML/CSS layout, with all text kept as selectable text. You can then open it in a design or word-processing program and make final corrections. If it cannot render the text accurately, ask for a labelled layout plan and place the exact text yourself in your own template.

## What you can change

You may safely change the title, learner group, topic, page size, orientation, colour palette, illustration theme, school branding, and output format. Keep the exact-text rule, compact-title rule, clear section order, print-economical colour use, readable typography, and final text-and-layout checks because these rules protect the accuracy and usefulness of the infographic.
