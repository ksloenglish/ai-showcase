# Grammar Explainer Video: Script, Scene Plan and Captions

## What this makes

The AI will turn your grammar-book pages into a complete plan for one short explainer video, led by a friendly virtual study buddy. You get a word-for-word script, a scene-by-scene plan of what appears on screen, a short quiz, English and Traditional Chinese captions, and YouTube text. It is for secondary students and includes a teacher check table.

## What you need

- Photos, a PDF or pasted text of the grammar-book pages for the unit.
- The exact unit title, for example "The present and the past". If you do not give one, the AI will ask.
- Optional choices you can change: the student level, the name of the study buddy, your school name, the video length and the caption language.

## How to use it

1. Prepare the material listed above.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Read through the result before giving it to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me plan a short grammar explainer video for [YEAR GROUP, e.g. Hong Kong secondary students] at [SCHOOL NAME]. The video is presented by a friendly, trendy virtual study buddy called [STUDY BUDDY NAME]. Address the viewers as "[SCHOOL NAME] students".

### Materials
I have provided: [ATTACHED OR PASTED GRAMMAR-BOOK PAGES].
The exact unit title is: [UNIT TITLE].
Use the grammar content from these pages. You may simplify, reorder or add a short explanation to help students understand and remember the ideas. You do not need to use every example. Do not invent grammar rules, and do not change the book's grammar terms without telling me.

### Task
Create an approval-ready plan for ONE video of about [4-5] minutes on this unit. Plan for about 2.4 spoken words per second, and allow extra reading time on screen. Clarity matters more than keeping to the time limit, and going over [5] minutes is fine if nothing would be rushed. The video must make the grammar very easy to understand and remember, with eye-catching but simple motion graphics (for example formula blocks, timelines, labelled diagrams, numbered rows, colour coding).

### Requirements
1. Title: show "[SERIES NAME] + [UNIT TITLE]" only. Never write a unit number, episode number or class label anywhere (title, speech, captions, YouTube text), so the video stays usable year after year.
2. Use this running order, adapting the number of grammar levels to the unit: logo and title opening; short friendly welcome and promise; a hook (a question or example that is answered later); an overview map of the whole system; one section for each real grammar point (form first, then uses, then contrasts or exceptions); a short break between the two halves; a quiz; a "pause and screenshot" cheat sheet; a closing goodbye; a final black screen with no sound.
3. Assume students have NOT read the book. Add a short on-screen label for every speech bubble, example, list or chip row so viewers know what it is about (for example "Habit words that go with the simple present"). Explain any visual code, such as which colour or bar means "long action" or "short action".
4. Turn tables and lists into visual structures, not paragraphs. Use formula blocks such as [am / is / are] + [verb] + [-ing], timelines, comparison pairs, and numbered rows with one example and its signal words. Use a consistent colour meaning throughout and list it in a colour key.
5. Highlight only the words being taught, never a whole sentence.
6. Give viewers time to read. After each list or tip, allow at least 1.5 seconds of reading time. Do not solve a crowded scene by shortening it or speeding up the narration.
7. Quiz: write 4 questions with enough context for only ONE correct answer. In each, show a blank line, a 3-2-1 countdown, then the answer with a short reason (grammar point plus the clue). Include an answer key.
8. Recap and closing card: list only the genuine grammar points from the book pages. Teaching devices (a mnemonic, a map, a "zone" sign, a quiz name) are not grammar points and must not appear in the recap, closing card or YouTube chapters.
9. Narration: write natural, bubbly but clear spoken English for a young presenter, short sentences, no tongue-twisters. Mark any word whose pronunciation could be unclear, such as names or a final "-ed" sound, so it can be checked when recorded.
10. Language: use British English spelling and single quotation marks.

### Output
Provide, in this order:
1. A one-paragraph plan: title, length, running order and a small "what changed from the book" table (any shortened, replaced or added example, with the reason) for me to approve.
2. A scene-by-scene table with these columns: scene number; purpose or book point; on-screen content and labels; exactly what the presenter says; presenter position or expression; approximate seconds; colour or highlight notes.
3. The quiz with answer key and the reason for each answer.
4. The cheat-sheet text and the closing-card list of grammar points.
5. Captions as two separate tables of numbered lines with start time, end time and text: English, and Traditional Chinese in standard written Hong Kong Chinese (not Cantonese), with English example sentences kept in English. Both must have identical timings. Do not suggest burning them into the picture.
6. YouTube text: a title in the form "[UNIT TITLE] | [SERIES NAME] | [SCHOOL NAME or CHANNEL]", a description that greets the students, lists the real grammar points, mentions the quiz and cheat sheet and states the two caption languages, chapter times based on the plan, and suitable tags. Do not mention AI or tools.
Use British English.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- Every grammar rule and example comes from my pages or is clearly marked as an addition in the change table.
- No unit number, episode number or class label appears anywhere.
- Every scene with a bubble, list or diagram has a plain on-screen label.
- Each quiz question has exactly one correct answer, and the answer key matches.
- The recap, closing card and chapters list only real grammar points.
- The English and Chinese captions have the same number of lines and the same times, the English matches the script word for word, and no line is longer than about two lines of screen text.
- The total of the scene times roughly matches the length you gave, with reading time included.
- Spelling is British and quotation marks are single.

If you cannot read an attached file, or cannot create something I asked for (for example the video itself, a voice recording or timed caption files), say so before you begin and give the closest useful alternative, such as editable tables I can paste into Word or a caption editor. Do not claim to have made a video, recording or file you have not made.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing (for example the unit title or the pages). Otherwise, proceed. Do not start building any video until I have approved the script.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Check that the AI has used the book pages you gave it and has not added incorrect grammar.
- Check that the quiz questions have only one correct answer and that the answer key is right.
- Read the Chinese captions yourself, or ask a colleague to. Make sure they are written Chinese, not spoken Cantonese.
- Check that no unit number has slipped into the title, narration or YouTube text.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Most AI chat assistants cannot make the finished video or a voice recording. This prompt gets you a script and plan that you can approve first. You can then give it to a video tool, a screen-recording tool such as slides with narration, or a colleague who makes videos. If it cannot produce caption files, ask for editable tables and paste them into a caption editor.

## What you can change

You can change the text in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`: year group, school name, study buddy name, series name, video length and the number of quiz questions. Keep these rules unchanged because they protect the quality of the video: no unit numbers, a label on every example, enough reading time, quiz questions with only one answer, and a recap that lists only real grammar points.
