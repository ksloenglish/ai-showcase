# Picture Writing-Task Video: Bring the Pictures to Life

## What this makes

A short animated video (about 70 seconds) that helps students picture what happens in a four-picture writing task before they write. It uses different camera angles for each picture, has no narration, and leaves the main characters and the story's key mysteries open for students to imagine. The AI first gives you a plan and asks questions, then makes the video, or a full shot-by-shot production pack if it cannot make video.

## What you need

- A photo or scan of the writing task: the instructions and all four pictures.
- Your school or channel logo (a PNG with a clear background works best), if you want an end card.
- Your choices on a few points. The AI will ask about them, so you can also answer later:
  - the look of the video (for example 3D animation, anime or worksheet style);
  - which characters or things must stay open for students to imagine;
  - whether you want on-screen words such as a speech bubble.

## How to use it

1. Prepare the material listed above.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Read through the result before giving it to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create a short visualisation video for [YEAR GROUP, e.g. S2] students in [PLACE, e.g. Hong Kong] who are at about CEFR [LEVEL, e.g. B1]. The video helps them understand and imagine a picture-based writing task before they write.

### Materials
I have provided: [THE WRITING TASK: INSTRUCTIONS AND FOUR PICTURES] and [OPTIONAL: LOGO FILE].
Use only what you can see and read in these materials. Do not invent story events that are not in the pictures, and do not leave out anything shown in a picture.

### Task
Work in two stages.

STAGE 1 – PLAN FIRST. Do not make anything yet. Send me a short plan and ask me any questions you need answered. The plan must include:
- two or three suitable visual styles, with your recommendation and one sentence on why;
- a shot list for each picture, with the camera angle or movement, what the viewer sees and what they hear;
- how you will keep the main characters and the key mysteries open for students to imagine;
- how you will add any on-screen words (for example a speech bubble or a final question), and whether the sound will be audio only or audio plus on-screen words;
- the total length and a rough time for each picture;
- which steps you can do yourself and which I would need to do in another tool.
Wait for my answers before Stage 2.

STAGE 2 – MAKE IT. After I reply, make the video (16:9, about [LENGTH, e.g. 1 minute 10 seconds]). Show me any key still images or sample frames for approval before you build the full video.

### Requirements
1. Follow the order of the four pictures. Give each picture several shots from different camera angles (for example a wide establishing shot, a low angle, a follow shot from behind, a close-up of hands, a point-of-view shot, an overhead shot), so each picture has enough coverage and the video feels varied. Do not show picture numbers on screen unless I ask.
2. Cover every important detail named in my notes: [LIST THE KEY DETAILS FOR EACH PICTURE, e.g. Picture 1: a packed hall, fans holding fan signs and light sticks; Picture 2: the performers on stage with a lit backdrop, an excited crowd; Picture 3: something on stage catches fire and a fan shouts a line shown in the picture; Picture 4: a question mark with a message].
3. Keep the main characters open to students' imagination. Never show the faces of the narrator ("me") or their friend. Use backs, hands, shoulders, point-of-view shots or distant figures. Make them gender-neutral: for example oversized hoodies in two different colours and bucket hats that cover hair, ears and neck. Do not give them names, ages or personalities.
4. Keep other mysteries open if the task has them. [E.G. Is the performer one singer or a band? Keep performers mostly backlit and in silhouette, with no clear faces. What exactly catches fire? Show only a fire from a distance, near the lights and backdrop, without revealing what burns. Who shouts? An unidentified fan seen from behind.]
5. Keep the story age-appropriate and calm. If there is danger, show it only up to the point in the picture. Show no panic, stampede or injury. Let the final shot continue the action of Picture 3 (for example the performer still singing while the fire spreads), then go to Picture 4.
6. Make the scenes physically sensible and consistent:
   - Seated people sit on the seats, not sandwiched inside them.
   - Everyone in the audience faces the stage, including the two friends. Never show the main characters facing the other audience members.
   - Fan signs and banners show their words to the stage and the performers. When seen from behind, they must be plain, with no words and no mirrored letters. When a sign is unrolled to show the audience, show the words clearly and keep them readable and stable in every frame.
   - Light sticks must look like real glowing sticks with handles, not glowing cards or rectangles.
   - Crowds must not be frozen. Give seated fans small natural movements such as swaying sticks, small waves, turning to talk to a neighbour, or clapping. Avoid every person doing the same thing at the same time.
7. Sound: no narration. Use real-sounding event audio: a big crowd (not a few whispering people), cheering, chanting, the performance, the fire crackling and any line shouted in the pictures. Anything that sounds like speech or chanting must be clear English and must not sound like another language. Performers sing without words or use simple English words. Keep the shout clearly audible over the music. Balance the volume so that no section is much louder or quieter than the others.
8. Text on screen: make all words (speech bubble, "What happened next?", end card) as clean graphics in an editor or design tool, not as AI-drawn text, so the spelling and letters are sharp and correct.
   - Speech bubble: a professional comic-book style, with a smooth outline, a soft shadow, a curved tail pointing at the speaker, and the words centred exactly inside (equal space above and below). It pops in as the line is shouted, stays long enough to read, then fades out.
   - Picture 4: a warm paper-coloured background like the worksheet, with the question mark from the worksheet and the question below it, close to the question mark with a small gap. Add a short suspense sound, not music that tells students what to think.
9. Start with a gentle fade-in from black.
10. End card (only if I gave a logo): after Picture 4, show my logo and the line "A [SCHOOL OR CHANNEL NAME] Production" on a dark, softly glowing background. Keep both modest in size, with plenty of empty space around them, centred as one group, and fade them in gently with a soft shimmer sound. Do not stretch or redraw the logo.
11. Use British English for all text.

### Output
Stage 1: the plan and your questions, in a short Markdown message.
Stage 2: the finished MP4 video. If you cannot create video yourself, give me a production pack instead: (a) a shot-by-shot storyboard table (shot number, camera angle and movement, what is seen, sound, length), (b) a ready-to-paste prompt for each shot for an AI video tool, with the consistency rules above written into every prompt, (c) a list of sound effects to find or generate, (d) exact wording and layout for every on-screen graphic, and (e) simple editing instructions.
Optionally, at the end, give me a YouTube title, a description with chapter timestamps for the four sections, and a note to turn on the "altered or synthetic content" label.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- every important detail in each of the four pictures appears, in the right order;
- no face of the narrator or their friend is visible in any frame (check frame by frame, including the first and last frames of every shot);
- the audience, including the two friends, faces the stage in every shot, and signs show words only to the stage side;
- all words on screen are correctly spelled and readable, and the speech bubble text is centred;
- light sticks look like sticks, seated people sit properly, and crowds are not frozen;
- the chanting, shouting and crowd sounds are clearly English and sound like a large crowd;
- the video has the fade-in, the Picture 4 layout and, if requested, a modest-sized end card;
- the total length is close to the length I asked for.

If you cannot read an attached file, create the requested file type, run a required check, or verify a layout, say so before proceeding. Offer the closest useful alternative. Do not claim to have completed a capability you do not have.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Watch the whole video, with the sound on, before you share it. Pause on every shot to look for faces, odd hands, wrong spellings and anyone facing the wrong way.
- Check that each picture of the task appears, in order, and that nothing in the video gives away an ending you wanted to leave open.
- Listen to the crowd, the chant and the shout. Make sure they sound like English and are clear.
- If the AI could only make a production pack, check each shot prompt before you use it in a video tool.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Ask it for the shot-by-shot storyboard table and the ready-to-paste shot prompts instead. You can use them in any AI video tool, then join the clips in a video editor and add the words and logo yourself.

## What you can change

You can change the year group, level, place, length, visual style, the list of key details for each picture, which mysteries stay open, the school or channel name and whether you want a logo end card. Please keep these rules as they are, because they protect the quality of the activity: no faces for the narrator and friend, everyone facing the stage, signs showing words only to the stage side, clear English sound, clean on-screen words, and no frightening content.
