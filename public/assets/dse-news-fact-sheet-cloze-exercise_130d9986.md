# DSE News Fact-Sheet Cloze Exercise

## What this makes

This prompt creates a HKDSE English Language cloze activity from a news article image, PDF, or pasted text. It gives you a checked transcription, a student Fact Sheet with 15 blanks, a student answer key, and detailed teacher notes.

## What you need

- A clear news article image, PDF, screenshot, or pasted article text.
- An optional Word template if you want a particular worksheet layout.
- The settings in **[SQUARE BRACKETS]** below, if you want to adjust the year group, word count, or language variety.

## How to use it

1. Prepare the article material and, if needed, your Word template.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in **[SQUARE BRACKETS]** if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Read the transcription and answer key before giving the activity to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create a HKDSE English Language Fact-Sheet cloze exercise for [HONG KONG SENIOR-SECONDARY STUDENTS / YEAR GROUP].

### Materials
I have provided: [A NEWS ARTICLE IMAGE, PDF, SCREENSHOT, OR PASTED ARTICLE TEXT].
Use only information that you can read from these materials. Do not invent facts, paraphrase source content, silently correct wording, or leave out source content.

### Task
Complete the two steps below. Use [BRITISH ENGLISH] unless I specify another language variety.

#### Step 1 — Transcribe the article
Transcribe the main article body and its in-article headings or subheadings word for word. Preserve the original wording, spelling, capitalisation, punctuation, and paragraph breaks.

Include only the article body and its in-article headings. Exclude photo captions, sidebars, advertisements, QR codes, URLs, page numbers, issue dates, publication mastheads, and other unrelated page material.

After the transcription, add a heading called **Notes on visibility:**. Write “All lines were legible.” or identify any unreadable material with [unclear] or [section not visible].

Before moving to Step 2, check the transcription against the source. Confirm that:
- every article section has been included;
- the paragraphs in each section have been included;
- the first and last words of each section match the source; and
- all columns have been read in the correct order, if the article has more than one column.

If this check shows a gap, correct the transcription before creating the cloze exercise.

#### Step 2 — Create the Fact-Sheet cloze exercise
Using only the transcription, write a concise and accurate Fact Sheet about the article. Use clear section headings and bullet points. Each bullet point must start with a bold-italic sub-label, followed by a natural and coherent explanation.

The Fact Sheet must be approximately [200] words, with a tolerance of ±10 words. Its language should be mainly CEFR B2 and suitable for HKDSE students.

### Requirements
1. Put this instruction immediately above the Fact Sheet title: “Based on the information in the news article, complete the following text by writing ONE word in each blank below. Your answers must be grammatically correct.”
2. Use this title format: **Fact Sheet: [TITLE OF THE ORIGINAL ARTICLE]**.
3. Create exactly 15 numbered blanks. Each answer must be a different, single word taken directly from, or grammatically derived from, the article.
4. Write every blank as a superscript number, followed by the first letter of the answer and at least 20 underscore characters. For example: ²v____________________.
5. Keep the answers in the same order as their intended source occurrences in the article. If a word appears more than once, identify and use the occurrence that belongs in the sequence; do not automatically select the first occurrence.
6. Select only B2, C1, C2, or unlisted words. Do not use A1, A2, or B1 words as final answers. Check each final answer form against a reliable CEFR word list available to you.
7. Include exactly five grammatical word-form changes. For every transformation, check the CEFR level of the final answer form, not only the source form. Reject any transformation whose answer form is A1, A2, or B1. For example, do not use a B1 noun simply because the source verb is B2.
8. Keep the words around each blank as close as possible to the source article’s original collocation, so that the context gives students a fair clue.
9. Do not let section headings, sub-labels, point titles, or nearby wording give away an answer. They must not contain the answer, its root form, or an obvious synonym. Use neutral labels instead.
10. Keep the content accurate, natural, and coherent. Avoid placing several blanks together without enough context for students to work out the answers.

### Output
Provide the result in this order.

#### Step 1 — Transcription
Give the complete transcription, followed by **Notes on visibility:**.

#### Part 1 — Cloze Passage (Student Version)
Present the Fact Sheet with the task instruction line, title, section headings, bullet points, and all 15 blanks. Do not show the answers in this part.

#### Part 2 — Answer Key (Student Version)
Give a numbered list of the 15 answers, one per line. Add a CEFR level in brackets only when it has been checked against a reliable word list. If a word does not appear in that list, omit the level rather than guessing.

#### Part 3 — Detailed Answer Key (Teacher Version)
Give a four-column Markdown table with these headers:

| # | Answer | Form Change | Original Context (from source article) |

For each item, give the answer, state “No change” or explain the word-form change, and quote a short source phrase with the source word in bold.

After Part 3, include a brief **Final Verification Checklist**. State the result of each check below:
- the transcription includes all sections, paragraphs, and columns from the source;
- the Fact Sheet has [200] words (±10), stating the exact count;
- there are exactly 15 different answers;
- the answers are in the correct source order, including the correct occurrence of any repeated word;
- exactly five answers are genuine word-form changes, and list them;
- every final answer form is B2, C1, C2, or unlisted; no final answer is A1, A2, or B1;
- the student key and teacher key match all 15 blanks;
- no heading, sub-label, or point title gives away an answer; and
- for each blank, the exercise wording preserves the source collocation as closely as possible.

If you cannot read the supplied material, check CEFR levels, create the requested file type, or complete one of the checks, say so clearly before proceeding. Offer the closest useful alternative, such as an editable Markdown table or plain-text worksheet. Do not claim that a check has been completed when it has not.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

Check that the transcription matches the article, especially where the source has several columns. Check the 15 answers against the student passage and confirm that the headings do not reveal them. If your AI provides word levels, review any word that is not clearly supported by the word list it used.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Ask it for an editable Markdown table or plain-text worksheet instead. You can then copy the content into your own Word template.

## What you can change

You may change **[HONG KONG SENIOR-SECONDARY STUDENTS / YEAR GROUP]**, **[200]**, and **[BRITISH ENGLISH]**. Keep the requirements for a complete transcription, 15 answers, source order, B2–C2 or unlisted answer forms, five transformations, preserved collocations, and no answer leakage unchanged because they protect the quality of the activity.
