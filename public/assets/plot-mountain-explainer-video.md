# Plot Mountain Explainer Video

## What this makes

A short animated video (about one minute, 16:9) that teaches students the four stages of a short story: exposition, rising action, climax and resolution. The reader is shown as a hiker climbing a "plot mountain". The video explains what the writer does and what the reader feels at each stage. It needs no handout, and it has no example story.

## What you need

- Optional: a picture of a plot diagram or plot mountain you already use. The AI will use it only as a guide and will redraw a clean, original version.
- Your students' level, for example "Form 2, CEFR B1".
- A few choices you can change: the length, the hiker (for example a girl or a boy), the narrator's voice and accent, and a small brand name to show at the start (for example your school or channel name).

## How to use it

1. Prepare the material listed above.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Read through the result before giving it to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create a short animated teaching video for [LEARNERS, e.g. Form 2 students in Hong Kong at CEFR B1 level]. The video teaches the plot mountain (plot diagram) and the four stages of a short story.

### Materials
I have provided: [A PLOT DIAGRAM PICTURE FOR REFERENCE / NOTHING].
If a picture is provided, use it only as a guide to the idea. Do not copy or reuse it in the video. Create a new, clean and professional diagram that matches the style of the video.

### Task
Create a 16:9 video, with narration, that shows reading a story as climbing a mountain. Start with a quick overview of the plot mountain and its four stages. Then show the reader as a hiker climbing the mountain. For each stage, explain what the writer should do and what the reader experiences. The video must explain everything by itself. I do not want any accompanying handout, worksheet or notes.

### Requirements
1. Before you build anything, tell me your plan (scenes, length, style, voice) and suggest a suitable length. I would like about [ONE MINUTE]. Ask me questions if anything is unclear, and wait for my answers.
2. Use exactly these four stages, in this order, with these names: exposition, rising action, climax, resolution. Do not rename them or add a fifth stage. You may add short supporting words, such as "turning point" for climax.
3. Opening overview: say that reading a story is like climbing a mountain. Draw the plot mountain step by step with two axes labelled "Level of excitement" (up) and "Sequence of events" (across). Show the four numbered stage labels in the right places: exposition at the low start, rising action on the way up, climax at the peak, resolution on the way down.
4. The reader is a hiker ([A GIRL / A BOY]). Keep the same character, with the same clothes and look, in every scene. Tell students clearly that the hiker is the reader and that the writer builds the path.
5. Use one scene for each stage:
   - Exposition: an easy, sunny walk through a meadow. Writer: introduces the characters and the setting, and adds a hook to catch the reader's interest. Reader: wants to know more.
   - Rising action: a windy, rocky climb. Writer: adds a conflict (a problem) and makes it harder, step by step. Reader: gets more and more excited.
   - Climax: reaching the summit and celebrating. Writer: creates the turning point, and the main character faces the problem. Reader: feels the most excited and "reaches the top".
   - Resolution: walking down to a campfire at dusk. Writer: shows how the problem is solved and how the character has changed. Reader: relaxes and feels the story is complete.
6. Show these ideas on screen as short "The writer…" and "The reader…" cards with one or two bullet points each. They should appear at the same time as the narration. Add a small map in the corner that shows where the hiker is on the mountain.
7. Give each stage its own colour and use the same colours in the diagram, the badges and the cards. End with a quick recap of the whole mountain and one closing line, for example "Build a mountain for your reader to climb!"
8. Language: British English. Use short, clear sentences and vocabulary suitable for [CEFR LEVEL]. Explain any harder word (for example "conflict") in simple words. The narration should be about [150] words for a one-minute video.
9. Voice: [BRITISH FEMALE], friendly, clear and not too fast. Add gentle background music and light sound effects, but keep the narration easy to hear.
10. Style: bright, friendly, flat-vector illustration that appeals to teenagers. Use a clean, professional look. All on-screen text must be spelt correctly. Add real text on top of the pictures instead of asking an image generator to draw words.
11. Branding: at the very start, above the title, show a small, neat tag reading "[BRAND NAME]". Fade it out with the title.
12. Do not include an example story. Do not use my uploaded picture directly.
13. Show me the full narration script and the design plan first. Wait for my approval before you make the final video.

### Output
Provide [A 16:9 MP4 VIDEO FILE WITH NARRATION, MUSIC AND ON-SCREEN TEXT]. Deliver the video only.
If you cannot make a video file, provide instead a scene-by-scene storyboard in a table with these columns: scene, time, what is on screen, narration, on-screen text, picture description (written so I can paste it into an image generator), and music or sound notes. Then give simple steps to build the video in a free editor such as Canva or Clipchamp.
Use British English.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- The four stage names are spelt correctly and appear in the right order, and the diagram axes are labelled correctly.
- Every "writer" and "reader" point in requirement 5 is covered in both the narration and the on-screen cards.
- The narration matches the approved script word for word, and the vocabulary suits [CEFR LEVEL].
- The hiker looks the same in every scene.
- The total length is close to what we agreed, and the video is 16:9.
- There are no spelling mistakes in any on-screen text, and no text is hidden behind other objects.
- The video does not copy my reference picture and contains no example story.

If you cannot read an attached file, create the requested file type, run a required check, or verify a layout, say so before proceeding. Offer the closest useful fallback. Do not claim to have completed a capability you do not have.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Watch the whole video with sound on. Check that the narration matches the words on screen.
- Check that the four stage names are spelt correctly and that each stage says what the writer does and what the reader feels.
- Check that the hiker looks the same in every scene and that no text is covered by a picture.
- If the AI could not make a video, check the storyboard and script carefully before you build the video yourself.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Many chat assistants cannot make a finished video. Ask for the storyboard table and the narration script instead. You can then paste the narration into a text-to-speech tool and the picture descriptions into an image generator. Assemble the video in a free editor such as Canva or Clipchamp.

## What you can change

You can safely change the learners and level, the length, the hiker, the narrator's voice and accent, the brand name, and the scene settings (for example, a different landscape). Keep the four stages in the same order, keep the writer and reader points for each stage, and keep the "script first, then approval" step. These protect the accuracy and quality of the lesson.
