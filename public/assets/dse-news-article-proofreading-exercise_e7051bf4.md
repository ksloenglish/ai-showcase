# DSE News-Article Proofreading Exercise

## What this makes

This makes a three-part HKDSE English proofreading worksheet from a news article. It includes a student version, an answer key, and a short word-source record for the teacher.

## What you need

- A clear article image, PDF, Word document, or pasted text. It must include the main body and section headings.
- An editable Word template if you want the worksheet to match a particular layout.
- Your choices for `[LEARNERS]`, the word-level standard, the minimum level, and any changes to the usual item totals.

## How to use it

1. **Prepare** the article and, if needed, an editable Word template and word-level list.
2. **Open** your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the materials.
3. **Copy** the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. **Send** the prompt. Check the finished worksheet before giving it to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create an HKDSE English Language proofreading exercise for [LEARNERS, e.g. Hong Kong S5–S6 students].

### Materials
I have provided: [DESCRIBE THE ARTICLE, PLUS ANY WORD TEMPLATE AND CEFR OR VOCABULARY LIST].
Use only information you can read from these materials. Do not invent, alter, or silently omit source content.

### Task
Create a three-part proofreading exercise based on the supplied news article. Students must identify and correct one inappropriate word in most lines of three short, coherent summaries. Use [BRITISH ENGLISH].

### First: transcribe and check the article
1. Transcribe the main article body word for word before making the exercise. Keep the original capitalisation, punctuation, paragraph breaks, headings, and any bold introductory blurb between the title and the first body paragraph.
2. Do not include photo captions, advertisements, sidebars, QR codes, URLs, mastheads, dates, page numbers, or other page furniture.
3. Add a short “Notes on visibility” statement after the transcription. Say whether every line was clear and identify any unclear or missing part.
4. Before drafting the exercise, compare the transcription with the source. Check that it has every named section, the same number of paragraphs in each section, the correct first and last words in each section, and all columns in the correct reading order. If the source is unclear, ask me for a clearer copy instead of guessing.

### Plan the three parts
1. Count the words in the introduction and every named section.
2. Part 1 must cover the introduction and the first named section only. Parts 2 and 3 must cover all remaining sections exactly once, in non-overlapping groups of one or two sections. Aim for broadly similar source reading lengths across all three parts.
3. List the section or sections used in each part. Do not reuse a section, skip a section, or bring an idea from a later section into an earlier part.
4. Write each part as one clear, natural summary paragraph. It must read as a connected paragraph, not as a list of separate sentences.
5. Keep the order of all selected answer words, and the ideas around them, in the same order as in the article. The context for each answer must come from the same paragraph or an adjacent paragraph in the same section.

### Create the correction items
1. Use exactly [15] numbered correction lines plus one worked example in Part 1. The worked example must be the very first row and must use the earliest selected answer word in the article.
2. Include one no-error line in each part. Put a tick in its correction column. Put the three tick lines in different positions, and never put a tick in the first row.
3. Choose [15] different answer words from the article. Each answer must be one word, must appear in the article exactly as recorded in the word-source record, and must be used in the grammatical form required by the exercise sentence.
4. Use [VOCABULARY STANDARD, e.g. an attached CEFR list] to select B2–C2 words and useful unlisted words. Do not use A1, A2, or B1 answers. If you cannot check the named list, say so and label the level “not verified” instead of guessing.
5. Include exactly [5] genuine word-form changes, with at least one in each part. Count a change only if the source form would not be grammatically correct or natural in the exercise sentence. If both forms work, rewrite the sentence to force one form or choose another word.
6. Put exactly one wrong word in every non-tick line. It must be one word only and have the correct grammatical form for its place in the sentence, but it must be clearly wrong or opposite in meaning. Do not use a near-synonym or a closely related word.
7. Keep the correct answer out of the same printed line as its wrong word. Do not reuse an answer word.
8. Keep the article’s immediate word partnership around the target word wherever possible. Use the same word or words directly before and after the target when this does not make the answer obvious or make the sentence unnatural. Keep the original order of words in any list from the article.
9. Test every corrected line. With the answer inserted, it must be complete, grammatical, clear, logically accurate, and consistent with the article. Do not omit needed objects, complements, or content words. Keep grammatical number consistent within any list. Capitalise the correction if it starts a sentence.

### Set out the worksheet
1. Make three parts with six visual rows in Part 1 and five visual rows in each of Parts 2 and 3. Let the paragraph text flow naturally across the rows; do not write one separate sentence for every row.
2. Ensure no two wrong words are in the same printed row. Do not leave a short final row. If the supplied Word template is used, each table row must show one visual line only, with no extra wrapping or overflow.
3. In the student worksheet, underline only the wrong word in the worked example. Do not underline any word in the other student rows.
4. In the answer key, underline the wrong word in every non-tick row and place the correction in the correction column. Show only a tick in each no-error row.
5. If I supplied an editable Word template, provide one Word document using its structure: student worksheet first, answer key second, and word-source record third. If I did not supply a template, give matching editable Markdown tables instead.

### Output
Provide:
1. The verbatim transcription and visibility notes.
2. The student worksheet and answer key in [A WORD DOCUMENT USING THE ATTACHED TEMPLATE / EDITABLE MARKDOWN TABLES].
3. A word-source record with exactly 16 entries: the example plus Lines 1–15. For each entry, give the final answer, the exact form in the article, whether it is a word-form change, and its vocabulary level or “not verified”.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- the transcription includes all main-text sections and paragraphs in the right reading order, and clearly flags any part that could not be read;
- Part 1 uses only the introduction and first named section, and every remaining section is used once only in Parts 2 and 3 with broadly balanced reading lengths;
- there are exactly [15] numbered lines, one first-row example, three tick lines in different non-first positions, and exactly [5] valid word-form changes across the three parts;
- all 15 answers are unique, directly sourced from the article, in article order, in the correct grammatical form, and distributed through the article;
- every wrong word is a single word in the correct form, clearly wrong rather than similar in meaning, and is the only error in its line;
- the answer is not visible in the same printed line, the article’s nearby word partnership and list order are kept where possible, and the corrected sentence is complete, clear, grammatical, and faithful to the article;
- no two wrong words share a printed row; the worksheet has six rows in Part 1 and five rows in Parts 2 and 3 without a short trailing row or unwanted line wrapping;
- only the example is underlined in the student version; every non-tick wrong word is underlined in the answer key; and
- the word-source record has 16 accurate entries in article order, with correct source forms, word-form-change status, and levels.

If you cannot read an attachment, make or inspect a Word file, check the stated word list, or confirm the final table layout, say so before proceeding. Give me the closest useful alternative, such as editable Markdown tables and a short list of checks I need to make myself. Do not claim to have completed a check you cannot perform.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Check the transcription against the article, especially its headings, first and last words, and any small print.
- Check that each article section is used once only and that the answer list follows the article order.
- Check that every answer works in its sentence, and that no answer can already be seen in the same line.
- Check the student worksheet, key, and word-source record against the item count, word-form-change count, and underlining rules.
- If you asked for a Word file, open it and check that the table rows and page layout look right.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Ask for editable Markdown tables and a word-source table instead. You can copy them into your own Word template and use the checks above.

## What you can change

You may change `[LEARNERS]`, `[VOCABULARY STANDARD]`, the language variety, the minimum word level, `[15]`, and `[5]`. Keep the rules about accurate transcription, section use, answer order, word forms, clearly wrong words, source wording, and final checking: these rules make the worksheet reliable.
