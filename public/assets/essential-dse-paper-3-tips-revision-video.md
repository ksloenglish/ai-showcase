# Essential DSE Paper 3 Tips: Revision Video

## What this makes

A short, narrated revision video (16:9) for senior secondary students preparing for HKDSE English Language Paper 3, reminding them of essential formal-letter conventions and common mistakes. Each tip gets its own clearly designed screen, with a voice-over, a "wrong to right" comparison and your school or channel branding.

## What you need

- Your logo as a PNG file with a transparent background (for example, `[YOUR-LOGO.png]`).
- Your revision notes, a summary sheet or a screenshot listing the points you want to cover. The AI should use it only as a source of ideas. The sheet itself must not appear in the video.
- A list of the tips you want to emphasise. The prompt below uses six sample tips about letter greetings, closings and common spelling mistakes. Replace them with your own if you wish.
- Your choices for the video title, branding name, maximum length, voice style and school year group (all in the prompt in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`).

## How to use it

1. Prepare the material listed above.
2. Open your chosen AI assistant and attach or paste the material.
3. Copy the whole prompt below. Change only the words in `[SQUARE BRACKETS]` if needed.
4. Send the prompt. Read through the result before giving it to students.

## Copy-and-paste prompt

```text
You are helping me create a short narrated revision video for senior secondary students in Hong Kong who are preparing for the HKDSE English Language Paper 3 (Writing) exam.

### Materials
I have provided: [A REVISION SUMMARY / SCREENSHOT / NOTES LISTING THE POINTS TO COVER] and [MY LOGO AS A TRANSPARENT PNG].
Use the revision material only as a source of ideas. Do not show the original sheet or screenshot in the video. Do not invent rules that are not in my material or that are not standard formal-letter conventions.

### Task
Create an English video titled "[Essential DSE Paper 3 Tips]" with "[K S Lo English]" branding. It should remind students of essential concepts and common mistakes in formal letters and emails. Choose a clean, modern style that suits teenage learners: a dark navy background with a yellow and white colour scheme, large readable text and simple animation. Present each tip as one screen. Show the incorrect form crossed out beside the correct form, with the changed letters highlighted. Phrase each tip as a short, friendly, memorable line. Rewrite my rough points in better wording where needed.

Cover these tips, in this order (replace with your own if needed):
1. Match the closing to the greeting. Use "Yours sincerely" when you know the reader's name (for example, "Dear Mr Chan"). Use "Yours faithfully" when you do not (for example, "Dear Sir/Madam").
2. Begin a formal letter with "I am writing to ..." (not "I am writing a letter to ...").
3. Use capital letters in greetings: "Dear Editor," and "Dear Sir/Madam," (not "Dear editor," or "Dear sir/madam,").
4. Use only the last name in a greeting: "Dear Mr Chan," (not "Dear Tony Chan," or "Dear Mr Tony Chan,").
5. Spell "writing" correctly: "I am writing to ..." (one "t", not "writting").
6. It is "Yours faithfully / Yours sincerely", with an "s" (never "Your faithfully / Your sincerely").

### Requirements
1. Use British English spelling and punctuation, including single quotation marks in on-screen text.
2. Keep the total video length to [45] seconds at most. Keep the narration brisk so that every tip fits.
3. Structure: a short title opening screen, then one screen per tip (labelled "TIP 1" to "TIP 6" with a small progress indicator), then a short closing screen.
4. Branding: show the logo inside a small yellow rounded square next to the name "[K S Lo English]" in the top-left corner of every content screen. Keep the logo comfortably small, with clear space between it and the edges of the logo square and the screen. Use the logo file exactly as supplied. Do not redraw it, and only resize it proportionally.
5. Narration: use a warm, upbeat, clear British English voice (young female teacher style). Read the title and each tip once. Do not add extra words. Make sure each spoken sentence finishes cleanly, with no stray breath or leftover syllable at the end of any line.
6. Timing and screen layout:
   - Show on-screen items at the moment they are spoken, and never earlier than the narration needs.
   - If a tip has a short reminder note (for example, "Keep a formal tone throughout" on Tip 2), show it as soon as the main text box appears and keep it on screen for the rest of that tip.
   - For a tip that has two correct forms and one incorrect form (Tip 1 and Tip 6 style), show the correct forms first and the incorrect form soon afterwards.
   - After the narration for each tip ends, hold the screen for about one second before moving on, so students can read it. Hold the closing screen for about one and a half seconds.
7. Layout details: put the "TIP" label clearly above the headline with a comfortable gap, so the two never look crowded. Do not add decorative arrows between the wrong and right forms. Do not add any unnecessary text, such as an explanatory note that is not in my material.
8. Closing screen: show a short encouraging line, such as "Get these right, and you'll write with confidence.", spoken and shown on screen. Do not add a separate branding voice-over or a branding text label to this final screen. The logo remains in the top-left corner like every other content screen.
9. Add soft background music that stays well below the voice-over, and a light sound effect when the correct form appears.

### Output
Provide the finished 16:9 video file (MP4) at 1080p if possible, together with the full narration script and a scene-by-scene list showing what appears on screen and when.
Use British English.

### Check before delivery
Check that:
- Every tip matches my material and the usual rules for formal letters. In particular, "Yours sincerely" is paired with a named person, and "Yours faithfully" is paired with "Dear Sir/Madam" or an unknown reader.
- Every on-screen word is spelt correctly in British English, and the spoken words match the script.
- There are exactly [6] tips in the order given, each with its label and progress indicator, and the video is within the length limit.
- The logo is the one I supplied, is sized comfortably inside its yellow square, and appears in the same position on every content screen.
- No narration line ends with a breath or a half-word, and there is a short pause after each tip before the next begins.
- The original sheet or screenshot does not appear anywhere in the video.

If you cannot read an attached file, create a video file, run a required check or verify a layout, say so before proceeding. Offer the closest useful fallback, for example a complete script and storyboard, or a slide deck with speaker notes that I can record myself. Do not claim to have completed a capability you do not have.

Ask one focused question only if an essential input is missing. Otherwise, proceed.
```

## Before you use the result with students

- Watch the whole video with the sound on. Check that each spoken line matches the text on screen and that every correct and incorrect example is shown the right way round.
- Check that the closings are paired correctly with the greetings (named person: "Yours sincerely"; unknown reader: "Yours faithfully").
- Check that the logo is the one you supplied and looks neat in its yellow square.
- Check the video length and that the narration does not end with a stray breath or half-word.

## If your AI assistant cannot make the file you want

Many chat assistants cannot produce a finished video. Ask for a scene-by-scene storyboard and a narration script instead. You can then turn them into a video with a slide tool (such as PowerPoint or Google Slides) and a text-to-speech or recorded voice-over.

## What you can change

You can change the title, the branding name and logo, the maximum length, the number and wording of the tips, the colour scheme and the voice style. You can also change the year group, or swap in a different set of formal-letter points from your own notes. Keep these rules unchanged, because they protect the quality of the video: correct examples shown beside incorrect ones, narration that finishes cleanly, a short hold after each tip, the unmodified logo, and a final check of the spelling and letter conventions.
